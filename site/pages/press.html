Fact sheet

  • 120 large petroleum tanks in South Portland, run by six major oil companies, sit close to homes, schools and child care.
  • State fence-line monitoring has now measured the air at the tank farms. At Citgo on Mechanic Street in Pleasantdale, the air consistently and dramatically exceeded the state standard for benzene, a well-studied carcinogen.
  • At South Portland Terminal on Lincoln Street, one reading registered 12 times the state’s ambient air quality standard, which is meant to protect people from cancer risk. Its highest monitor averaged about 4.5 times Maine’s guideline, the largest exceedance in the state.
  • 10 of 11 monitored facilities statewide had at least one fence-line monitor above Maine’s ambient air guideline for benzene. Portland Pipe Line in South Portland was the only one fully within it, and its tanks are largely empty. The two worst are both in this city.
  • Citgo announced an emissions-reduction plan on Oct. 28, the day after the Council workshop. It pledges better detection and monitoring and faster infrastructure work, without published targets, deadlines or independent verification.
  • Next workshop planned for Tuesday, Dec. 1 at 6:30 p.m. in City Hall Council Chambers, 25 Cottage Road. Staff and special counsel return with options on emission controls, a local ordinance or licensing approach, a building moratorium near two facilities, and more monitoring.
